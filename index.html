Getting started

Two ways in — your call

Clara is a pair: an app on your phone and a backend on your PC. Start with whichever is in front of you.

Path 1 · PC first

Set up your computer

  1. On your PC, run the installer below. It takes 20–40 minutes (mostly downloading the model) and ends with a QR code.
  2. Scan the QR code with your phone's camera to get the app.
  3. Open the app and enter the computer address and pairing code the installer showed.

Lost the code? Run clara pair for a new one. Need the QR again? Run clara app.

Path 2 · App first

Start on your phone

  1. Install the APK from #download below and open it.
  2. Tap "New to Clara? Set up your computer first".
  3. The app shows you the requirements and the install command (with Copy) — and can send the setup link to your computer.

Then run the installer on your PC, come back, and pair.

The one-line PC install
git clone https://github.com/TheWiderLensInitiative/clara ~/clara && cd ~/clara && ./install.sh

Run it as your normal user — it asks for your password once. It checks your hardware, downloads the Bonsai 2 27B model, sets up Clara's sandboxed computer, private search, and voice, then prints your computer address and pairing code. Everyday commands: clara status, clara pair, clara app, clara logs, clara update.

Android app (Kotlin) ──Wi-Fi / Tailscale──▶ Clara Bridge (Python/FastAPI, your PC)
                                                   │
                                        Laya router decides in ~⅓ second
                                        ┌──────────┴──────────┐
                                  quick chat            real task
                                        │                   │
                              Bonsai 2 27B (PrismML)   Hermes agent (sandboxed,
                              on your NVIDIA GPU       no sudo, its own workspace)
Privacy

Private by design, not by promise

By default, nothing leaves your PC. Not your chats, not your files, not your memories. Data only leaves your machine when you explicitly ask it to:

  • ✓
    Private search — queries go to your own SearXNG instance running in Docker on your PC.
  • ✓
    Cloud boost — only if you enable it, only the task you send, capped by the spending limit you set.
  • ✓
    Connectors — only the services you connect, only the data those tasks need.
  • ✓
    Phone dictation — may use your phone's built-in speech recognizer.
  • ✓
    Passwords stay on your phone — stored in the Android keystore behind your fingerprint. Clara can use a login without ever seeing it.

The full policy: clara.thewiderlens.info/privacy

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is Clara really free?

Yes. Clara is free and open source under the Apache 2.0 license. No subscription, no account, no paywalled features. The optional cloud boost uses your own OpenRouter key with caps you set — that's the only thing that could ever cost money, and it's off by default.

I installed the app first — now what?

Open the app and tap "New to Clara? Set up your computer first." It shows the PC requirements and the one-line install command with a Copy button, and can send the setup link to your computer. Run git clone https://github.com/TheWiderLensInitiative/clara ~/clara && cd ~/clara && ./install.sh on your PC, then come back and pair.

Does Clara work without the internet?

Your chats, tasks, voice, and memories all run locally on your PC — no internet needed for those. Web search, connectors, and remote access from outside your home (via Tailscale) need a connection, naturally.

How is this different from ChatGPT or Claude?

Those run on someone else's computers; your conversations live on their servers. Clara runs on your hardware. Your chats, files, and memories never leave your PC unless you explicitly send them somewhere.

What phones are supported?

The Clara app is Android 9 and up. The backend runs on an Ubuntu 24.04+ PC (26.04 works) with an NVIDIA GPU (12 GB+ VRAM).

Can I see the source code?

Absolutely — that's the point. The whole project is on GitHub under Apache 2.0: the Android app, the Python bridge, the agent setup, the router training data, and the installer.
